Lightning, forecasts & early warning

Dates May 2024 – Nov 2026
Role Geospatial Data Engineer (until July 2026, Developer – Data Analyst)
Stack Python, xarray, Zarr/STAC, PostGIS, Kafka, FastAPI/TiTiler, MapLibre
Visit keraunos.co

Keraunos is a lightning-research and detection company that operates the LINET network in Colombia. I contribute to Predicthor, its real-time platform that relates lightning to asset monitoring and weather forecasts, and I designed its geospatial and weather data architecture: a cloud-native stack (Zarr, STAC, COG, TiTiler) that I proved in SIMK and the team then adopted. Predicthor is private, so this page describes the work; SIMK has its own write-up with screenshots.

Forecast data. I re-architected the ECMWF IFS pipeline from GRIB into an analysis-ready Zarr datacube with a STAC catalog: sub-grids merged without interpolation, separate chunk layouts for maps and time series (one chunk read instead of 90) and publishing without downtime. On top of it I built a FastAPI/TiTiler service that packs physical values into 24-bit RGB tiles and decodes them on the GPU.

Satellites. GOES-16/19 pipelines that turn all 16 ABI bands into Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs, with per-band calibration and cloud-cover alerts for each asset.

Spatial accuracy. In a multi-model precipitation ensemble (ECMWF, GFS, WRF) I found that WRF's irregular latitude spacing shifted its northern edge by 8.2 km. I moved the computation onto the true coordinates and implemented conservative regridding to a common 0.1° grid.

Real time. I took MPSR, the company's model of lightning-induced outage risk for each transmission tower, into real-time operation on Kafka, and presented it at AtmosCol 2025. I also built the services that turn data from Keraunos' electric-field sensors and field announcers into alerts, and worked with the team on the alerting engine so its state survives restarts and every notification is sent once. Spatial SQL in PostGIS measures lightning exposure around every asset in a single batched query.

Web mapping. I designed and built SIMK, the Keraunos Meteorological Information System, almost single-handedly, from the interface to the backend: model fields rendered as WebGL textures in MapLibre, vector tiles from PostGIS and a Docker stack. Read the case study.
