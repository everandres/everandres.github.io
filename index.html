Ever Andrés Salazar

Environmental engineer, geospatial and climate data engineer

I'm an Environmental Engineer who found my way into data engineering through maps. For more than six years I've worked with satellite, weather and spatial data, and I care about one thing above the rest: that every number can be traced back to where it came from.

At Keraunos, a lightning-research and detection company, I'm a Geospatial Data Engineer. I designed the geospatial and weather data architecture behind Predicthor, its real-time platform that relates lightning to asset monitoring and weather forecasts, and I designed and built SIMK, its internal weather system. Before that I spent three years at IDEAM's Forecasting and Alerts Office (OSPA), handling the hydrometeorological data behind Colombia's national early-warning system.

I co-founded Orbis Geoscripting, a school where we taught programming for cartography to more than 150 professionals from ANLA, National Natural Parks and IDEAM. I graduated with academic honors and a Meritorious Thesis from Universidad Distrital Francisco José de Caldas (GPA 4.0/5.0), and in 2025 I was lead speaker at AtmosCol, presenting an early-warning system for lightning-related events that affect infrastructure.

When I'm not immersed in maps and code, my interests range from literature (I published my first book of short stories in 2023) and the history of ancient Egypt to salsa and hip-hop. I'm also fascinated by fashion design, and I'm an avid viewer of shows like BoJack Horseman. That mix of interests shapes how I approach my work.

What I work on

Weather and climate data

Pipelines for forecast models, satellites and station networks: from raw GRIB and NetCDF to analysis-ready Zarr datacubes, PostGIS and tile services, checked and validated along the way.

Cartography and GIS

Spatial analysis, web maps and visual stories, from a census of 1.47 million city trees to 26 years of El Niño over a whole country.

Teaching

Python, SQL and JavaScript for cartography, taught to more than 150 professionals. I still enjoy explaining technical methods in plain language.
